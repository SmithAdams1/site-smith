What changed: the property route is gone

The single most important fact, and the one most out-of-date articles still get wrong: the direct real-estate purchase route to the Golden Visa closed in October 2023. Buying an apartment no longer qualifies for the Golden Visa on its own. Any content promising "buy property, get residency" is describing a route that no longer exists. Property remains an excellent investment in Portugal; it is simply no longer a Golden Visa qualifying route.

The stay requirement is light

Unlike a residence-by-work route, the Golden Visa carries a minimal physical-stay requirement, commonly summarised as around seven days in the first year and roughly fourteen days per subsequent two-year period. This is the feature that makes it suit investors who do not intend to relocate. It is the opposite of the D2 entrepreneur visa, which requires genuine residence in Portugal.

Residency, and the path to citizenship (which is moving)

  • Permanent residence is available after five years of legal residence, a threshold reported as unchanged so far.
  • Citizenship is the part in flux. Portugal's 2025-2026 nationality reform is widely reported to lengthen the naturalisation clock from five years to around ten years in general, and about seven for nationals of EU and Portuguese-speaking (CPLP) countries, counted from the first residence permit. The exact in-force date, the final law text, and the grandfathering cut-off for existing applicants were still being contested and finalised through 2026 (a Constitutional Court ruling and parliamentary re-approval were part of the process). Treat any specific citizenship timeline as provisional and confirm it against the Diário da República or a qualified lawyer before relying on it.

(Sources: Global Citizen Solutions, CCLEX, Lexidy nationality-law updates, 2026.)

Family included

A single Golden Visa application can include the main applicant's spouse or civil partner, dependent minor children, dependent adult children in education, and dependent parents. Note that Portugal's newer immigration law (Lei 61/2025) has tightened family-reunification rules more broadly, so confirm current conditions at application time.

Golden Visa or D2?

These two routes suit opposite profiles. The Golden Visa is passive capital with almost no stay requirement, for an investor who will not move. The D2 is for a founder or self-employed professional who will genuinely live in Portugal and run a business. Frequently asked questions

Can I still get a Portugal Golden Visa by buying property?
No. The direct real-estate purchase route closed in October 2023. As of 2026 the Golden Visa qualifies through non-real-estate routes such as regulated CMVM funds (from €500,000), cultural heritage (from €250,000) and eligible lower-density investment (from €200,000), administered by AIMA.

What is the cheapest Golden Visa route in 2026?
The lowest commonly cited threshold is around €200,000 for eligible lower-density or qualifying investment, and €250,000 for cultural-heritage support. The most used route is the regulated investment fund, from €500,000. Confirm exact figures with AIMA.

How much time must I spend in Portugal on a Golden Visa?
Very little: commonly around seven days in the first year and about fourteen days per subsequent two-year period. This is why it suits investors who do not plan to relocate.

How long until citizenship?
Permanent residence is available after five years. The citizenship timeline is being reformed and is reported to lengthen to roughly ten years (about seven for EU and CPLP nationals); the final terms and effective date were still being settled in 2026, so confirm before relying on a specific figure.

Is the Golden Visa better than the D2?
Neither is "better"; they suit different people. The Golden Visa is passive investment with a light stay requirement; the D2 is for those who will live in Portugal and run a business. See our side-by-side comparison.
