In one line: The Golden Visa and the D2 are Portugal's two main routes for non-EU investors, and they suit opposite people. The Golden Visa is passive capital with almost no obligation to live in Portugal. The D2 is for a founder or self-employed professional who will genuinely move and run a business. Choosing wrongly costs years, so the honest answer starts with what you actually intend to do.

What the D2 subsistence figure actually is

The D2 has no statutory minimum investment, but you must show means of subsistence set against Portugal's minimum wage (RMMG), not the IAS. As of 2026 that is 100% of the minimum wage for the main applicant, which is €920 per month, plus 50% for each additional adult and 30% for each dependent child. A common annual baseline cited for the main applicant is around €11,040 (twelve months at €920), though Portugal's fourteen-month salary convention and case-by-case consular assessment mean any single annual total is approximate. (Source: Portaria 1563/2007 and the MNE visa portal; 2026 minimum wage set by Decree-Law 139/2025.)

When the Golden Visa is the right route

Choose the Golden Visa if you want Portuguese and EU residency without moving your life. You keep your tax residency elsewhere, spend only a handful of days a year in Portugal, and hold the qualifying investment (most commonly a regulated fund) for the required period. It is the cleaner route for a busy professional or family whose base stays abroad. Since October 2023 it is no longer a way to "get residency by buying an apartment"; property is now a parallel decision, not the visa itself.

When the D2 is the right route

Choose the D2 if you intend to actually live in Portugal and build or run a business here, and you would rather commit time and a credible plan than lock up €500,000. There is no fixed capital threshold, which makes it far more accessible on paper, but the business plan is scrutinised for genuine viability and you must genuinely reside in the country. It rewards commitment, not passive capital.

What both routes share

Both lead to permanent residence after five years and include family (spouse or partner, dependent children, dependent parents). Both are also affected by Portugal's 2025-2026 reforms: the new immigration law (Lei 61/2025) tightened family reunification, automatic permit extensions ended after October 2025 so renewals must be proactive, and the nationality reform is widely reported to lengthen the citizenship clock to roughly ten years (about seven for EU and CPLP nationals). The precise citizenship timeline and its effective date were still being finalised through 2026, so treat any specific figure as provisional and confirm it before relying on it.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between the Golden Visa and the D2?
The Golden Visa is passive investment (from €500,000 in a regulated fund) with almost no stay requirement, for investors who will not relocate. The D2 is for entrepreneurs and self-employed people who will genuinely live in Portugal and run a business; it has no fixed minimum investment but requires a scrutinised business plan and real residence.

Which is cheaper, the D2 or the Golden Visa?
On paper the D2, because it has no fixed minimum investment, only adequate business capital and proof of subsistence (100% of the €920 minimum wage for the main applicant in 2026). The Golden Visa requires a qualifying investment, most commonly €500,000 in a regulated fund.

Can I get the Golden Visa by buying property?
No. The direct real-estate route closed in October 2023. Current routes are non-real-estate (funds, heritage, lower-density investment, research, business).

Do both lead to citizenship?
Both lead to permanent residence after five years. Citizenship is being reformed and the timeline is reported to lengthen (to roughly ten years, about seven for EU and CPLP nationals); confirm the current rule and effective date before relying on it.

How long must I stay in Portugal on each?
The Golden Visa requires only about seven days in year one and fourteen per two-year block. The D2 requires genuine residence, with no absence over six consecutive or eight non-consecutive months per permit period.
