# Investing in Portugal Real Estate in 2026: Yields, Hotspots and Net-of-Cost Returns

**In one line:** As of 2026, Portugal remains one of Europe's most durable property markets for international investors, backed by an upgraded sovereign credit rating, EU and euro membership, and year-round rental demand. The figure that matters is not the headline yield but the net-of-cost, euro-denominated return after taxes and management, and that is where most Portugal numbers fall short.

Smith & Adams advises international investors as an owner-operator group, not a brokerage. This guide sets out the 2026 investment case on the evidence: what is verified, what still has to be checked against a dated source, and how we measure a return once the costs are in.

## Why Portugal, in 2026

Portugal's appeal to foreign capital rests on a small set of facts that are easy to verify and hard to argue with:

- **An improving sovereign.** Portugal's credit rating was raised through 2025: S&P to A in February and A+ in August, and Fitch to A in September, on sustained debt reduction and budget surpluses. (Source: S&P and Fitch, 2025.)
- **Safety.** Portugal ranked 7th safest country in the world and 5th in Europe in the 2025 Global Peace Index.
- **EU, Eurozone and Schengen.** Investment is made and returns are earned in euros, inside the single market, with transparent title.
- **Tourism-driven rental demand.** Lisbon remains one of Europe's strongest urban hospitality markets, with year-round demand and a limited supply of professionally managed premium stays.

These are third-party markers, not our own numbers. They are the reason a Portugal allocation is a question of *which asset*, not *whether the country*.

## Residency, if that is part of the plan

Many international buyers pair a Portugal investment with a residency route. Two facts are essential and current as of 2026:

- **The direct real-estate purchase route to the Golden Visa closed in October 2023.** Buying an apartment no longer qualifies for the Golden Visa on its own. Current qualifying routes are non-real-estate: regulated CMVM investment funds (commonly from €500,000, with the fund at least 60% invested in Portuguese companies), cultural heritage (from €250,000), eligible lower-density investment (from €200,000), scientific research, and business or job creation. Administered by AIMA.
- **The D2 visa** is a separate, older route for entrepreneurs and the self-employed, and can suit investors building or running a business in Portugal.

## Tax in 2026: NHR is gone

The single most common mistake in older Portugal content is describing the classic Non-Habitual Resident (NHR) regime as available. It is not.

- **NHR was closed to new applicants on 1 January 2025** and replaced by the **IFICI** regime ("NHR 2.0", the Tax Incentive for Scientific Research and Innovation).
- **IFICI** offers a 20% flat rate on eligible Portuguese employment or self-employment income for 10 years, plus relief on certain foreign income, **but its eligibility is narrow**: highly-qualified professionals (degree at EQF level 6 or above, or a PhD) in science, technology and innovation, healthcare, green energy, R&D, and qualifying startups or exporters, and only for people who were not Portuguese tax residents in the prior five years. **It is not automatic for a passive property investor.**

For non-residents, a fiscal representative is generally required, which is one of the things Smith & Adams handles through its Tax Representation practice.

**Can I still get a Golden Visa by buying property in Portugal?**
No. The direct real-estate purchase route closed in October 2023. As of 2026 the Golden Visa qualifies through non-real-estate routes such as regulated investment funds (commonly from €500,000), cultural heritage (from €250,000) and eligible lower-density investment (from €200,000), administered by AIMA.

**Is the NHR tax regime still available?**
No. NHR closed to new applicants on 1 January 2025 and was replaced by IFICI, which has narrow eligibility focused on highly-qualified roles in science, technology, healthcare, green energy and R&D. It is not automatic for passive property investors.

**What does "net-of-cost return" mean?**
