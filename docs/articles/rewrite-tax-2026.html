NHR is gone. IFICI is not a replacement for investors.

The single most common mistake in older Portugal content is describing the classic NHR regime as available. It is not.

  • NHR closed to new applicants on 1 January 2025 and was replaced by IFICI, the Tax Incentive for Scientific Research and Innovation, often called "NHR 2.0".
  • IFICI offers a 20% flat rate on eligible Portuguese employment or self-employment income for ten years, plus relief on certain foreign income. But its eligibility is narrow: highly-qualified roles (a degree at EQF level 6 or above, or a PhD) in science, technology and innovation, healthcare, green energy, R&D, and qualifying startups or exporters, and only for people who were not Portuguese tax residents in the prior five years.
  • It is not automatic, and not designed, for a passive property investor. If your Portugal plan is buying and letting real estate, assume standard Portuguese tax rules apply, and model on that basis.

(Source: PwC Tax Summaries; International Bar Association overview, 2025/2026. Confirm eligibility with a qualified adviser.)

A 2026 change non-residents must check: the flat 7.5% IMT

Under Decree-Law 97/2026, non-tax-residents are reported to pay a flat 7.5% IMT on residential purchases regardless of value, with carve-outs (for example a refund if you become tax-resident within 24 months). This is recent legislation and its exact entry-into-force has been reported inconsistently between sources. Treat the 7.5% non-resident rate as current-but-confirmable, and check it against the Diário da República or a Portuguese tax adviser before you budget on it. It materially changes the acquisition cost for the international buyer this article is written for. (Source: Decree-Law 97/2026; confirm entry-into-force.)

Do you need a fiscal representative?

For non-residents, a Portuguese fiscal representative is generally required to hold property and file returns. It is a formal obligation, not an optional service, and getting it wrong creates avoidable friction with the Finanças. Frequently asked questions

Is the NHR tax regime still available in Portugal?
No. NHR closed to new applicants on 1 January 2025 and was replaced by IFICI, which has narrow eligibility focused on highly-qualified roles in science, technology, healthcare, green energy and R&D. It is not automatic for passive property investors.

What taxes do I pay when buying property in Portugal?
On purchase: IMT (progressive, with a reported flat 7.5% for non-residents under Decree-Law 97/2026) and Stamp Duty at 0.8%. Each year you hold: IMI (0.3%-0.45%) and, above €600,000 of taxable value, AIMI. Confirm current figures before budgeting.

How is rental income taxed for a non-resident?
As of 2026, residential rental income for non-residents is taxed at a 25% autonomous rate. Residents can access a 10% rate on qualifying longer-term "moderate rent" leases between 2026 and 2029.

How are capital gains on Portuguese property taxed?
Since 2023, residents and non-residents are treated the same: 50% of the gain is taxable at progressive IRS rates. The former 28% flat rate on the full gain for non-residents was abolished as discriminatory.
